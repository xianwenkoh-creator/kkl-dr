Sheets

Pens of the PDF (colour, line weight, dashed or solid) or layers of the DXF, with the number of closed outlines each draws. “Platforms” makes a platform from every closed outline in that pen or layer and gives it the level written inside it (two levels inside make a ramp); “Boundary” takes its largest closed outline. Pick the pen that draws the toe lines; berm and top-of-slope lines are usually a lighter or dashed pen.

Levels found on the design sheet are listed with their text; press “→ FEL” to use one for the selected zone.

Model
boundary: none, the platforms plus their slopes will be used

Platforms are drawn like zones, along the toe of the slope at formation level. The excavated surface is the lowest of every platform floor and of the batter rising from its edge under the rules, so adjacent platforms, sumps and ramps combine on their own. A platform with vertical sides is one retained by a wall (a sheetpiled pre-cut, for example).

Scale
not set

Best: an E/N coordinate grid on the sheet (read automatically). Otherwise the declared scale from the title block, checked with two clicks across a known dimension, a grid interval or the bar scale. A sheet printed at a different size than the title block says needs the calibration. With two surveyed coordinates (boundary corners from the worksite layout) the sheet is georeferenced: scale and rotation together, and pasted coordinate lists land in the right place.

Levels on the sheet
—

Spot levels (two or three decimals) snap to their survey marker; levels to datum (“-12.00m SHD”) are shown in blue where they are written. Click a dot in exclude mode to drop a wrong level. In add mode click a point and type its level for scans or missing text.

Zones and final excavation levels
ZoneFEL mNote

Draw a zone by clicking its corners on the base sheet; double-click or Finish to close. Every zone needs one final excavation level; use several zones for stepped levels.
